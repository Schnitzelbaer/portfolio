## The problem

Brightvale is a mid-sized digital bank. Its account opening flow had grown over five years of regulatory changes: 14 screens, three identity checks and a funding step that many people never reached. Only 54% of the people who started the flow made a first deposit.

<Callout icon="💡" title="Why it mattered">
  Every abandoned onboarding cost Brightvale roughly CHF 90 in marketing spend. The flow was the single biggest lever on growth.
</Callout>

## Research

I combined funnel analytics with twelve moderated sessions and a diary study with recent sign-ups. Three patterns came up again and again: people did not know how long the process would take, the identity check felt like a dead end when it failed, and the funding step asked for money before showing any value.

<Quote author="Participant 7" role="Diary study, week 1">
  I gave up at the ID photo. It kept saying "try again" but never told me what was wrong.
</Quote>

![Journey map with the three main drop-off points highlighted](./images/journey-map.jpg "Journey map of the original flow. The three drop-off points account for 80% of abandonment.")

<Decision title="Show the whole path up front">
  People abandoned because they could not see the end. Instead of hiding steps behind a progress bar, the new flow opens with a short overview of all six steps and how long each takes. Compliance agreed because nothing about the required data changed, only the order and framing.
</Decision>

## Design

The identity check was the hardest part. The vendor's SDK returned generic errors, so we built a small translation layer that maps each failure to a specific, fixable instruction.

<FigureGroup caption="Identity check with specific recovery hints, and the reframed funding step.">
  <Figure src="./images/id-check.jpg" alt="Identity check screen with a hint about glare on the ID card" />
  <Figure src="./images/funding.jpg" alt="Funding step showing the account benefits before asking for a deposit" />
</FigureGroup>

<Decision title="Test the overview against a checklist">
  We were not sure whether people preferred a visual overview or a checklist they could tick off. An A/B test with 4,000 new sign-ups settled it: the checklist performed better on small screens, the overview on larger ones. We shipped both, by breakpoint.
</Decision>

<Columns>
  <Column title="What we kept">
    The legal copy, every required data field and the vendor for identity checks. Changing any of these would have restarted a six-month compliance review.
  </Column>
  <Column title="What we changed">
    Order, framing and feedback. Most steps now explain why the data is needed, and every error says how to fix it.
  </Column>

## Outcome

<Outcome type="achieved">
  - Drop-off before the first deposit fell from 46% to 32%.
  - Median time to open an account went from 19 to 8 minutes.
  - Support tickets about identity checks halved.
</Outcome>

<Outcome type="expected">
  - Lower acquisition cost per funded account once marketing budgets are rebalanced.
  - Reuse of the error translation layer in the business account flow.
</Outcome>

![Account home after onboarding](./images/dashboard.jpg "Account home, the first screen after a successful deposit.")

<Toggle summary="Methods and sample sizes">
  Funnel analysis over 90 days (38,000 starts), 12 moderated sessions, a 2-week diary study with 9 participants and one A/B test with 4,000 sign-ups.
</Toggle>

<Reflection>
  - Involving compliance in the first workshop saved months later. They became co-authors of the solution instead of reviewers.
  - I would test the error messages earlier. They had more impact than any visual change.
  - Shipping two variants by breakpoint added complexity we now have to maintain.
</Reflection>
